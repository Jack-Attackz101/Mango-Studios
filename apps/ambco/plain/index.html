P-01 · Stave & partial arc

Propulsor bearings

Water-lubricated bearings manufactured in stave and partial arc systems for conventional and nuclear-powered submarines, naval surface vessels and other industrial equipment.

  • Dual-hardness, low-friction nitrile rubber staves approved by the U.S. Navy
  • Grooves between staves channel seawater through the bearing and carry away sand and abrasives
  • Partial arc (round bore) bearings held to exacting tolerances

P-03 · Self-lubricating

Thrust bearings

Tilting pad thrust bearings for propulsion shafting and industrial equipment, whether the need is small or over 30,000 pounds.

  • No lube oil pumping system or jacking pumps required
  • Self-lubricating in every operating condition, including jacking speeds
  • Self-aligning, load-leveling design allows shaft deflection while minimizing wear

04 — Legacy

A century in national security.

Fig. 1921
  1. 1921

    Los Angeles

    Mr. Wyllis founds American Metal Bearing at 3rd Street and Central Avenue, making specialty bearings for the earliest Indy cars.

  2. 1940s

    The Second World War

    AMB becomes the preeminent supplier of propeller shaft bearings to the navies of the world, a role it has held since.

  3. 1950s+

    The post-war fleet

    Frigates, destroyers, amphibious transport docks and nuclear submarines all run AMB components in their propulsion systems.

  4. 1973

    Garden Grove

    In April 1973 the company moves from downtown Los Angeles to its 50,000 ft² facility at 7191 Acacia Avenue.

  5. 85 yrs

    Primary Navy supplier

    Propulsion bearings for nearly every large surface combatant in the U.S. Navy, plus ice breakers, research vessels and submarines.

  6. Today

    The next century

    Research with Department of Defense offices on new propulsor concepts, under an AS9100D and ISO 9001:2015 quality system.

05 — Company

Who we are.

Our story

A small Los Angeles bearing shop in 1921. The primary supplier of bearing propulsion systems to the U.S. Navy today.

Through the twentieth century the company pioneered advances in journal bearing design. Since the Second World War it has engineered critical propulsion components for the marine and naval industries. In 1973 it moved to Garden Grove, California, where it remains.
